# Assignments and assessment

## How the course is assessed
| Component | Weight | How it is checked |
|---|---|---|
| Labs | 20% | after each notebook lab, type `Lab N done` in the course chat; your trainer records it |
| Assignments A1, A2, A3 | 15% (5% each) | marked with the rubrics below, 5 points each |
| Mini project (Day 3) | 15% | team demo, rubric in [day3.md](day3.md), section "Mini project" |
| Capstone (Day 5) | 40% | panel review, rubric in [day5_capstone.md](day5_capstone.md) |
| Final quiz (Day 5) | 10% | 20 multiple-choice questions |

A certificate is awarded at 70% or more.

## How to submit an assignment
* About 45 minutes each, done after the day's session.
* One page: Word, PDF, or a clear photo of handwritten notes.
* Submit the way your trainer announced on Day 1 (for example by e-mail or in the course chat), **before 09:15 the
  next morning**. Feedback is given the same morning.

## A1 (after Day 1): Model choice note
**Use case:** an internal assistant that answers HR policy questions for 8,000 employees of an Indian bank. HR data
must stay in India.

**Write one page with these four headings** (the same as in [day1.md](day1.md), section "Assignment A1"):
1. **Options considered:** at least three ways to use a model, one line each.
2. **Criteria and weights:** your criteria with a weight for each, adding up to 100%.
3. **Choice:** the option you recommend, in two or three sentences, with the main reason.
4. **Risk to watch:** the one risk that worries you most about your choice, and how you would notice it.

**Rubric (5 points):**
| Points | For |
|---|---|
| 1 | at least three realistic options |
| 2 | criteria and weights that fit this use case (data residency must carry real weight) |
| 2 | a choice that follows from the weights, and a risk with a way to notice it |

## A2 (after Day 2): RAG risks for your own use case
**Write one page with these three headings** (the same as in [day2.md](day2.md), section "Assignment A2"):
1. **Use case:** a knowledge assistant from your own work, in two sentences (who uses it, which documents).
2. **Five risks and controls:** a table with five rows: the risk, and one concrete control for it.
3. **First test:** the one risk you would test first, and exactly how (what question you would ask, what result
   would worry you).

**Rubric (5 points):**
| Points | For |
|---|---|
| 2 | risks specific to the use case, not generic |
| 2 | controls that are concrete and feasible |
| 1 | a first test that someone could actually run |

## A3 (after Day 4): One ADR for your capstone
**Write one ADR** with the headings of [adr_template.md](adr_template.md) (Context, Options considered, Decision,
Consequences, How we will know this was wrong, Exit plan), for the decision your capstone team finds hardest. See
[day4.md](day4.md), section "Assignment A3".

**Rubric (5 points):**
| Points | For |
|---|---|
| 1 | at least three real options compared |
| 2 | honest consequences, including the downside you accept |
| 2 | a measurable "how we will know this was wrong", and an exit plan |
